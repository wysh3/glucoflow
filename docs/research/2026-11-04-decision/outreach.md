# Pilot outreach scripts

Send only to people you know or who were introduced. Do not send patient data. Keep the first message short.

## WhatsApp to a doctor partner

> Dr [name], we built a small result receipt for hospitals. Every lab report gets a named clinician, a clock and a proof that the patient was told. No diagnosis, no risk score.
>
> We are looking for three hospitals for an eight-week pilot. The first step is a 30-minute meeting and a de-identified audit of 100 reports. Could you introduce me to the quality head at [hospital]?

## Email to a hospital quality head

**Subject:** A result register that is already auditable

> Dear [name],
>
> NABL 112A and NABH ask for a critical-alert register with the person informed, the time and a read-back. Most hospitals keep that on paper or across WhatsApp.
>
> We have built an administrative result receipt. Every incoming lab report gets a named clinician, a due clock, an acknowledgement, a patient communication record and a read-back for critical results. Every event is hash-chained. A receipt code can be verified without exposing a patient name or a test value.
>
> We are running an eight-week pilot with three hospitals. The first step is a 30-minute meeting and a de-identified audit of 100 consecutive reports. There is no cost for the audit.
>
> Would [date/time] work?
>
> [Name]
> [Phone] · [Website]

## Email to a lab director

**Subject:** Delivery proof for every report you send

> Dear [name],
>
> When a lab sends a report to a hospital, the lab's record ends at delivery. The hospital's acknowledgement usually lives in a phone call or a paper diary.
>
> We are building a neutral receipt code that a lab can issue and a hospital can acknowledge. The lab gets delivery proof. The hospital gets an accreditation register. The patient gets a receipt. No clinical interpretation.
>
> Could I show you a five-minute demo and ask what would make this useful to your lab?

## After the meeting

Ask for:

1. The last critical result that went wrong.
2. The current register and who maintains it.
3. The last NABH or NABL finding.
4. Permission for a 100-report de-identified audit.
5. The budget owner and the approval path.

Do not ask "would you use it". Ask for the audit and the pilot.

## Do not say

- "clinically proven"
- "AI diagnoses"
- "we have hospitals"
- "ABDM integrated"
- "thousands of users"
- "validated" before a paid continuation
