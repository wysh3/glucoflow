# Pilot letter of intent template

This is a template, not a legal contract. A lawyer should review it before signature.

---

**Result receipt pilot**

Between **[Hospital legal name]**, **[address]** ("the Hospital"), and **[glucoflow legal entity]** ("the Supplier").

## 1. Purpose

The Hospital will test the Supplier's result-accountability ledger for **[8] weeks**. The pilot covers incoming laboratory reports and the administrative receipt that records a named owner, a clock, clinician acknowledgement and patient communication.

## 2. Scope

- Up to **[100] consecutive reports** for the baseline audit.
- One clinic or quality team, one lab workflow.
- Synthetic or de-identified data for configuration.
- No clinical interpretation, diagnosis, triage, risk scoring or treatment recommendation.

## 3. Hospital responsibilities

- Name a quality owner and a clinical owner.
- Provide de-identified report records for the baseline audit.
- Give the Supplier access to the result workflow for the pilot period.
- Record acknowledgements and patient communication in the ledger.

## 4. Supplier responsibilities

- Configure the receipt workflow.
- Train the quality and clinical owners.
- Provide the weekly receipt report: unowned, overdue, median acknowledgement, critical read-back and patient informed.
- Protect data under the DPDP Act 2023 and the DPDP Rules 2025.
- Not contact patients without the Hospital's written instruction.

## 5. Commercials

- Pilot fee: **₹[amount]**, payable **[on signature / on completion]**.
- Continuation price after the pilot: **₹[5,000 to 20,000] per facility per month**, subject to a separate agreement.
- A continuation is not automatic. The Hospital may stop at the end of the pilot.

## 6. Data and IP

- The Hospital owns its patient and operational data.
- The Supplier owns the software and the receipt format.
- The Hospital may export the register and the survey pack at any time.
- Data is deleted or returned within **[30] days** after the pilot unless a continuation is signed.

## 7. Success measures

The pilot is successful if:

- Median time to acknowledge improves by at least **25%** over the baseline.
- Unowned results fall below **10%**.
- At least **90%** of critical results have a completed receipt with a read-back.
- The quality team uses the survey pack in its audit file.
- The Hospital signs a continuation or pays a continuation invoice.
